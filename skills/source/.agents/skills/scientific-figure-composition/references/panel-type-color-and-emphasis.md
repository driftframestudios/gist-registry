# Panel, type, color, and emphasis

Load for a composition whose geometry or styling can change the reading.
Aesthetics serves the evidence through coherent hierarchy, useful contrast,
controlled density, and deliberate relationships among marks and words.

## Compose a reading sequence

Start from the admitted comparison and its consequential qualification. Compare
at least three plausible compositions when they create materially different
reading burdens: an aligned single plot, small multiples, an overview/detail
pair, a matrix with annotations, or a figure plus exact table. Use one coherent
primary route; references and detail support the same question.

Give each panel a job. Panel area follows the data density, label length, and
comparison need rather than a uniform grid. Repeated panels share order,
semantic colors, and scales when their quantity and question permit it. Distinct
units stay visible. Insets name their bounds and scale so magnification remains
interpretable. A decorative panel receives space only when it supplies useful
context, explanation, or orientation.

An explanatory title states an admitted finding. An exploratory title names the
question or data relation. Annotation selects a supported feature and keeps
its comparison context: counts, denominators, tails, contrary observations,
and uncertainty. Direct labels can reduce legend lookup; a compact legend may
serve repeated categories better. Label the relationship that actually exists.

## Typography and space carry structure

Define a small, consistent type hierarchy for title, panel label, axis label,
ticks, annotations, and caption. Preserve scientific glyphs, minus signs,
superscripts/subscripts, and units. Align labels and repeated plot regions to
make reading order easy to follow. Use margins and spacing to group related
content and separate distinct claims. Keep line/marker weights sufficient at the
real output size, including fine intervals and low-contrast uncertainty bands.

A strict rule such as removing all gridlines can impede precise reading.
Compare restrained gridlines, direct value labels, and a recoverable table
against the reader task. Full-width layouts, dark backgrounds, rounded cards,
shadows, and ornamental imagery remain contextual presentation choices; they
carry no independent scientific authority. Quantitative geometry keeps its
scale and baseline intact under any chosen treatment.

## Color is a semantic vocabulary

Choose palette roles before assigning hues. Ordered magnitude needs an ordered
progression; a meaningful reference can support divergence; a periodic quantity
can support a cyclic palette. Categorical identities retain stable mappings.
Clipped ranges, missing data, selection, and uncertainty receive deliberate
roles. Neighboring panels preserve identical color meanings or explain changes.

Inspect the palette under relevant color-vision and grayscale conditions and
provide redundant labels, line styles, shapes, or accessible values where color
carries a necessary distinction. Simulation is a diagnostic observation, while
accessibility across users requires fitting receiver evidence. The colorbar's
unit, domain, reference, and under/over treatment are part of the figure.

## Evidence and counterevidence

The controlled **Declutter and Focus** study found differences in subjective
ratings and recall of highlighted patterns under its tasks. It also reports
no significant trustworthiness difference across the designs, and some people
read focused versions as having an agenda. Therefore selective emphasis is a
fitted design choice, not a trust intervention. Exploratory views may preserve
balanced attention when the important pattern remains unsettled.

Crameri and colleagues' perspective explains distortions caused by poorly
matched color progressions. Its recommended palette principles still require
a match to the variable's topology and the actual audience. Rougier and
colleagues provide practiced guidance about audience, message, and medium;
their rules are not a universal measured ranking of visual styles.
